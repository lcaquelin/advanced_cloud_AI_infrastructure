# Business case

Owner: ........

Steps 1 and 2 of the method. Margaux's first two questions: whose problem is it, and what does it cost us
today? What does your product change, and how will we know? Short lines; a page or two is enough.

## The pain

- Who has the problem (customers, couriers, store managers, support, marketing)? What happens today?
- What does it cost Popote, in euros, minutes or customers? Say where each number comes from.

## The decision your product changes

- Which decision is made differently with your product? Who makes it, and how often?
- What is done today without your product (nothing, a rule, a person)? That is your baseline.

## How we'll know it works

| | What | Today | Target |

| Business metric | e.g. customers still ordering 30 days later | | |
| Guardrail | e.g. coupons used by customers who would have stayed | | |

- How you'd prove it: an A/B test or a held-out group (who gets the product, who doesn't, for how long)?

## The value line

- In words: what you earn, minus what you spend. e.g. "leavers brought back × 100 € minus coupons used × 8 €".
- Your assumptions, each with its source (measured, from Popote's data, or assumed and why):

| Assumption | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |

- **Wrong by half:** halve the assumption you're least sure of. Does it still pay? If not, what would you need
  to prove first?

## Risks

- Personal data: which, where does it go, who sees it?
- Fairness: does it treat a group of customers worse (new customers, one district)?
- What if the AI or ML piece is wrong? What does the customer see?
